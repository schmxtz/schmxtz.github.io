Cognitive shuffle

Lie down and let a random word appear every few seconds. Picture each one briefly, then let it go. The words are deliberately unrelated, so your mind has nothing to build a train of thought on.

8 s

How it works

This is based on Luc Beaudoin's cognitive shuffle, also called serial diverse imagining. Picturing a stream of unrelated, neutral things is close to what the mind does as it drifts toward sleep. It also crowds out the worry and planning that keep you awake.

The list has over a thousand concrete, everyday words, chosen to be easy for non-native speakers too. They are drawn in a random order without repeats, two words from the same category never appear back to back, and your place in the list is remembered between sessions.

During a session: tap or press space to pause, press Esc or tap "stop" to end it.
